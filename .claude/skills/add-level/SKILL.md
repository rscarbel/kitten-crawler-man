# Add a Level or Tile

## Levels

A level is pure data: `LevelDef` (`src/levels/types.ts`) — `id, name, mapSize, roomMobs, hallwayMobs`, plus optional `bossRooms`, `nextLevelId` (chaining), `arrivalLoadingScreen`, `hasCollapseTimer`, `hasTreasureRoomGuards`, `numStairwells`, `isOverworld`, `hasSpiderLab`, `extraSpawns`, `onMobKilledSpawns`, `progression`. `MobSpawnRule`: `{ type, chance, minCount, maxCount, minLevel, maxLevel, config?, escorts?, terrain? }` (`terrain` limits an overworld rule to forest-edge or open-field points; see `add-creature`) where `type` is a fixed string union — new mob keys must be added to that union and registered in `spawner.ts` (see `add-creature`).

### Checklist

1. Create `src/levels/levelN.ts` exporting a `LevelDef` (copy `level1.ts`), including its `arrivalLoadingScreen` tips.
2. Register in the map in `src/levels/index.ts` (`getLevelDef` throws on unknown ids).
3. Chain it: set the previous level's `nextLevelId` to this id; omit `nextLevelId` on the terminal level.
4. Level-complete flow is automatic: `StairwellSystem` shows the descend menu (hidden when `nextLevelId` is absent), and `DungeonScene`'s `onDescend` wiring saves progress, emits `levelComplete`, shows `LevelCompleteScreen`, and does `sceneManager.replace(new DungeonScene(nextDef, ...))` carrying player snapshots, achievements, and abilities forward.

### Arrival loading screen

Every environment arrives behind a loading screen; nothing opts in. `LevelDef.arrivalLoadingScreen: { tips }` is required (a non-empty tuple), so a new level without tips does not typecheck, and building interiors show their floor's tips. The screen is an `ArrivalLoader` (`src/scenes/ArrivalLoader.ts`), which `GameplayScene` declares abstract: each scene `begin`s it last in its constructor (after everything that queues art or figures has queued it), mounts `arrivalLoading.surfaces()`, returns from `update` and `render` on it first, fades it out after its UI and `dispose`s it on exit. `SceneManager.replace` calls `assertReadyToEnter`, so a scene entered without `begin` throws on its first entry. Anything that announces the arrival (the floor's `level_begins` sting, its music, a room's playlist) is started through `arrivalLoading.whenClosed(...)`, never under the screen. Harnesses wait on the public `arrivalLoadingOpen` (`scripts/settleArrival.ts`). A non-floor environment sets `arrivalLoadingScreen.kicker` (the tutorial does). It goes up only when `floorArrivalOwesWork` says the arrival owes real work, so a walk out of a building, or into one already warm, goes straight to play. The work is `floorArrivalLoadTasks` in `src/scenes/floorArrivalLoad.ts` (painted environment art, the figure prewarm queue, the fetched sprite groups, then the ground chunks and decorations for the first view), run by a `LoadRunner` behind a `LoadingOverlay`.

What makes an arrival hitch-free is the content's own prewarm, not the screen: anything drawn from the first frame (a creature, an NPC, a machine that animates on its own) must call its `prewarm…` when it is placed, so the figure task drains it. `docs/asset-management.md` ("A heavy arrival goes behind a loading screen") has the rest. Gates: `npm run verify:arrival-load` (every environment: the screen goes up, nothing announcing the arrival starts under it, then zero render-path bakes, stand-ins, direct paints, chunk bakes and env steps in the first frames of play), `verify:loading-screen` (runner pacing, and every `GameplayScene` wired to its loader), `verify:town-arrival-load`, `verify:town-soak`.

### Residency: memory follows the party

A floor may hold far more art than fits in memory at once, as long as what it needs around the party does. Every resident cache gives memory back while the party stays on the floor (by idle time, distance or budget), never only at the stairs, and anything placed in the world whose art should follow the party registers a resident set with the scene's `WorldResidency` (`src/systems/WorldResidency.ts`). See "Memory follows the party within a floor" in `docs/asset-management.md`.

- **Buildings** register themselves: `DungeonScene` places every `SPRITE_BUILDING` tile's facade sheets with `environmentSheetPlacements` and holds them by distance through two `EnvironmentSheetResidentSet`s (`FACADE_RING`, `FACADE_LIFE_RING`). A new floor with buildings gets this without new code, provided its facades are runtime-painted building sheets (`BUILDING_KEYS`, `buildingLifeKeyOf` in `runtimeBuildingSheets.ts`).
- **Every other sheet** the floor's `spriteGroups` paint is placed over the whole map and held for the visit. **Ground materials**, including a region's own, and **indoor-only groups** (`interiorSpriteGroups`) are in no set: chunks touch the materials they draw, and the idle sweep returns the rest. A new kind of placed art with positions of its own (districts, regional props) gets its placements from where it is laid out and its own ring, as `EnvironmentSheetResidentSet` does for buildings.
- **A city's crowd** registers a `CrowdResidentSet` over its citizens where the crowd is built (the overworld registers the street crowd and the market's vendors beside `TownLifeSystem`). Its looks are pinned by distance, in a city and out of one; nothing pins a look on construction.
- **Scene-owned tile art** is released when the scene exits (`GameMap.releaseTileArt`); a scene that builds a map of its own, or a `TileChunkCache` outside `GameMap`, releases it in `onExit`/`dispose` too.

`npm run verify:environment-sheet-budget` measures the largest sheet set any position on every floor needs against the budget, so a new floor is checked automatically. To measure a new floor's residency, add its routes to `RESIDENCY_FLOORS` in `scripts/residencyRoutes.ts` (the routes, and the `compare` pair whose difference the report prints, the one that saw more first) and a driver beside `residencyFloor3.ts`, dispatched from `runResidencyRouteHere` in `scripts/residencyRouteProcess.ts`. Then `npm run report:residency -- --floor=<id>`. A good pair is a route that tours the floor and one that goes straight to the same spot and waits there past every idle window: what the tour still holds more of is what the floor fails to give back.

### Where the party is set down

Every arrival — a floor, a door, a stair, a building exit — places both crawlers with `findPartyArrivalTiles` (`src/map/findWalkableTile.ts`), never at a fixed offset such as `startTile.x + 1`. That tile can be a prop, a wall or a fence, and a crawler set down inside one cannot take a step.

### Spawn placement

`spawnForLevel(def, map)` fills room/hallway spawn points from the generated map. `extraSpawns` place mobs relative to a landmark, an `ExtraSpawnOrigin`: `{ kind: 'mapCenter' }` or `{ kind: 'bossRoom', bossType }`, the centre of the room seated for that boss.

### Map generation

`src/map/GameMap.ts` orchestrates; `DungeonGenerator` (rooms + L-hallways + boss/safe rooms), `OverworldGenerator` (the third-floor Taloncrest — used when `isOverworld`), `TutorialMap`. Special rooms (the spider lab) are gated by the LevelDef flags.

A progression floor is a list of `GauntletDef`s (`levels/types.ts`), each a boss (`GauntletBossType`, `src/map/bossRoomSizes.ts`) and a `route` to its gateway safe room: `{ kind: 'branches', branchCount, branchRooms, bathroomChoke? }` (parallel chains from the previous exit) or `{ kind: 'spine', rooms, splits, stealthGate? }` (one forced, winding chain from the previous gauntlet's exit; never the first gauntlet; always seats the nursery; the spider lab hangs off it). Boss rooms are sized per boss in `BOSS_ROOM_SIZE` and floored per boss in the generator's `BOSS_ROOM_FLOOR` table, both `Record<GauntletBossType, …>`. No boss has an arena: each fights in its own sized boss room, and nothing else stamps a fighting ring onto the map. Past the last gauntlet's exit is a free region, the only place stairwells stand (I3/I4 prove every stairwell needs both bosses). A spine-fed last gauntlet holds open ground beyond its exit (`SEGMENT_FREE_RESERVE`) while its spine is planned, so the free region has somewhere to grow.

A gauntlet may set `approach: { kind: 'flooded', minLengthTiles, widthTiles }` (`src/map/floodedApproach.ts`): its gateway-to-boss corridor is carved wide, at least that long, dry to its midpoint then flooded deepening to `MAX_WATER_DEPTH` at the door (`floodDungeonTile`), with a barrel-seat alcove a quarter of the way into the wet half and a trigger line just past it. Only the Kraken's gauntlet may set it, and it must: `GauntletDef` is a union on `bossType` that requires `approach` for `'kraken'` and forbids it for every other boss. Such a gauntlet takes its quest choke on the stem only. The built corridor is `DungeonData.floodedApproaches` (`FloodedApproachData`): its cross-sections, plus `bendCorners` for the outer corner a bend carves past any cross-section; `floodedTileDepths` is the one list of its tiles and their depths, which the flood, the keep-clear set and F1 all read. F1 in `validateProgression` checks length, width, water on every carved tile and the seat.

#### Dungeon water

Standing water is the river's model reused (`src/map/dungeonWater.ts`): a `FloorTypeValue.water` tile with a `waterDepth` and a `drainsTo` floor, so `GameMap.isWadeable` and everything built on it (slowdown, waterline clip, splashes) covers it unchanged. `floodDungeonTile` / `drainDungeonTile` change one cell; at run time go through `GameMap` instead, so the map tracks the tile and repaints it:

- `floodWater(tiles, depth)` floods dry floor or sets the depth of water already there.
- `drainWater(tiles, ticks)` lowers the tiles to dry floor over that many world ticks, advanced by `advanceWaterDrains()` once per world tick (`isDrainingWater` while one is under way); `dungeonWaterDepthAt(x, y)` reads a tile.
- The water layer is drawn by `src/map/tiles/dungeonWaterColumn.ts`, one depth sample per tile; a depth change invalidates it through `GameMap`.

`GameMapCheckpoint.dungeonWaterDepths` holds the depth of every tile that has ever stood under water. A restore puts each back and drains anything flooded since the capture; drains under way are dropped. Map water is not in `PersistedWorldState`: a page reload regenerates the floor with only its generated water, so a room dressing that floods or drains tiles re-applies its own water from its own persisted state on load.

### The overworld town

`OverworldGenerator` owns the wilderness (circus, forests, ruins, spawn scatter) but **not** the town's layout — it consumes a declarative `TownPlan` from `src/map/town/`. Before changing anything inside the walls, read **`docs/town.md`**: it covers the painter order, why the street hierarchy is a list order rather than a priority rule, the centre-relative offsets that must be re-tuned when the layout moves, and the `OverworldData`/building-id invariants that quests key off.

Building interiors are data too: one layout file per building in `src/map/town/interiors/` (props from `TOWN_INTERIOR_PROPS`, anchored at the footprint's north-west tile), applied by `GameMap.generateInterior`. Every building is keyed by its `BuildingId` (`src/map/buildingIds.ts`, `<location>_<what it is>`), never by the name on its sign, which is `buildingDisplayName(id)` and read only for text. A prop a save remembers (a breakable or a searched container) is placed with `statefulProp(x, y, propId, id)`, its id unique within the room. The "Interiors" section of `docs/town.md` has the prop contract, occupant anchoring, breakables and the density rule. Gates: `npm run gates:town-interior-props`, `verify:interiors`, `verify:interior-interactions`, `verify:interior-payout`, `verify:interior-density`.

Verify layout changes at **`?townmap`** (localhost) — the town is several screens wide, so no in-game screenshot can show whether one worked.

### Briar Hollow and keep-outs

Every floor-3 world also has **Briar Hollow**, the ratkin village east of the town: sited by `src/map/overworld/briarHollowSite.ts`, laid out from the authored template in `briarHollowLayout.ts`, stamped by `paintBriarHollow.ts`, and checked by `briarHollowChecks.ts`. Its record is `gameMap.briarHollow` (null on every other map). The durable description — siting, layout contract, roofless walls, palisade segmentation (a save-format contract), the hostile-only gate, the siege flow field and persistence — is the "Briar Hollow" section of **`docs/town.md`**. `?townmap` cycles town → world → Briar Hollow.

Any wilderness pass that places something (rivers, forests, ruins, camps, cliffs, spawn scatter, bounty sites, boulders, fairies) must stay off the landmarks laid out before it by asking one `KeepOut` (`src/map/overworld/keepOut.ts`, disc and rect shapes with `contains(x, y)`), never by restating a geometry test. A new landmark is a new shape added to the keep-out, not an edit to every pass. `npm run verify:briar-hollow-site` proves the village over 200 seeds.

Village tile types (`tileTypes.ts`): `HOLLOW_WALL` (roofless, neighbour-aware, Y-sorted), `HOLLOW_THRESHOLD`, `HOLLOW_PLANK_FLOOR`, `HOLLOW_PROP_LOW` (solid, sight-transparent) / `HOLLOW_PROP_TALL` (solid, blocks sight) — which prop is keyed by `spriteKey` (`hollow:<propId>` on the drawing tile, `hollow_part:<dx>,<dy>` on the rest of the footprint) — `HOLLOW_DECAL`, `HOLLOW_PALISADE` / `HOLLOW_PALISADE_GAP`, `HOLLOW_GATE` (walkable by type; hostiles are turned away by the `BLOCK_HOSTILE_ONLY` runtime flag, which `GameMap.isWalkableForHostile` reads), `ROCK_DEPOSIT`, `PASTURE_GRASS`, `CROP_FIELD`. Tile painters are handed the grid, not the map, so village painters read the site through `hollowSiteRegistry.ts`.

### Circus grounds and the Starlight Tent

The circus grounds use the same pattern. They field no enemies, and the floor's spawn scatter keeps off them. `circusGroundsLayout.ts` is the authored template, and `paintCircusGrounds.ts` stamps it. The seed never reaches geometry, and blocking dressing only goes on the r12–14.5 rim. The site record is `gameMap.circusGrounds`, and painters reach it through `circusSiteRegistry.ts`. Tile types: `CIRCUS_LOT` (walkable ground), `CIRCUS_STRUCTURE_TALL` (blocks sight) and `CIRCUS_STRUCTURE_LOW` (seen past). Both structure types are keyed `circus:<id>` on the drawing tile and `circus_part:<dx>,<dy>` on the rest. The "Circus grounds" section of **`docs/town.md`** lists every registry a Y-sorted multi-tile solid must join; use it as the checklist for the next one. The Starlight Tent's interior is always the maze, open from the start. The "Starlight Tent" section covers the maze interior's drapes, floor marks, lighting, its fairness render order, and the Act III hall of mirrors (the generated light-puzzle board in `src/map/starlightTent/`, one per world seed × difficulty). Gates: `npm run verify:circus-grounds`, `gates:circus-art`, `verify:starlight-tent`, `verify:mirror-board`, `verify:difficulty-guard`, `gates:starlight-tent-art`.

### The clogged bathroom (floor 2)

Floor 2's Current Goblin gauntlet seats a **clogged bathroom on every branch**, at waypoint `bathroomBranchIndex(len)` (`Math.floor(len / 2)`, `src/map/gauntletLayout.ts`), asked for by the gauntlet's branches route `bathroomChoke: { w, h }` (`level2.ts`, `BATHROOM_ROOM_W`/`_H`). The planner returns `GauntletPlan.bathroomRooms`, moves a safe-access pocket that would hang off a bathroom to the room before it, and the room is added with the `'bathroom'` `RoomRole` (`regionMap.ts`): no mobs, no generic decor, no crawler signs, its own `clogged_bathroom` character.

- **Layout** is one pure function, `layOutBathroom` (`src/map/bathroomLayout.ts`), returning `BathroomRoomData` on `DungeonData` and `gameMap.bathroomRooms`: `bounds`, `centre`, `branchIndex`, `entranceTile`/`entranceDoorway`, `blockedDoorways`, four 2-wide `stallDoorTiles` on the north face, two `sinkTiles`, `pipeWallTile` (wall face) plus `pipeWallStandTile`, `plumberTile`, `queueTiles` (head first, out through the primary blocked doorway and `QUEUE_CORRIDOR_SPILL_TILES` into the corridor), `doorwayKnots` for every other blocked doorway, and `loiterTiles` for after the fix.
- **Blocking** is a runtime flag, not the goblins: `BLOCK_BATHROOM` on every blocked doorway, queue and knot tile (`bathroomBlockedTiles`), honoured by `GameMap.isWalkable` only while `gameMap.bathroomPlumbingState` is `'broken'`. `setBathroomPlumbing(state)` flips every copy at once; the key set survives `rebuildBlockedMasks`, and `GameMapCheckpoint.bathroomPlumbing` carries it through a rewind. A crowd alone cannot block (players shove mobs, and validators and A* ignore entities).
- **`isWalkableIgnoringBathroomQueue`** is `isWalkable` as if the plumbing were fixed. Use it for questions about the route as built (which rooms lie past a safe room) and for shots in flight (a missile or stone meets the goblins as bodies, not as a wall); never for where a crawler may step.
- **Progression:** S6 in `validateProgression` (expected through `hasBathroomChoke` in `progressionExpectations`): one bathroom per branch at the halfway index, the gateway unreachable with every bathroom rect removed, and each entrance reachable without crossing another bathroom.
- **Runtime:** `CloggedBathroomSystem` (`src/systems/cloggedBathroom/`, `scene.cloggedBathroom`) owns the queue of `BathroomGoblin`s, the plumber, the floor's one pipe board, the stalls and the "The Clogged Bathroom" mini quest; the solved state is checkpointed and persisted (`PersistedWorldState.cloggedBathroom`).
- **Dev:** `npm run playtest -- bathroom` (spawn `bathroomEntrance`), `?pipeboard`.
- **Gates:** `npm run verify:clogged-bathroom` (map: blocked while broken, every copy opens, the player never shut out of the stalls, pipes or plumber, checkpoint keeps the state), `verify:clogged-bathroom-play` (headless scene: timer held by the board and explainer, every weapon on the goblins pays nothing and gets an "ow", companions ignore them, the fix opens the gateway, stall visit and Refreshed, checkpoint/save/old-save/death rewind), `verify:pipe-board`, `render:clogged-bathroom`, `verify:progression`. Each takes `--fault=` and must go red on every fault.

### The stealth gate and the prison (floor 2)

Floor 2's spine seats a **stealth gate room** at chain index `stealthGateChainIndex(roomCount)` (`Math.floor(roomCount / 2)`, `src/map/spineLayout.ts`), asked for by the spine route's `stealthGate: { w, h }` (`StealthGateRequest`; `level2.ts`, `STEALTH_GATE_ROOM_W`/`_H`). It is a `SpineRoomKind` of `'stealth_gate'` and is added with the `'stealth_gate'` `RoomRole` (no character dressing, its own lighting tag and floor). The planner keeps everything else off it: the nursery never sits on or beside it (`touchesGate`), no lane forks on a link touching it, the spider lab never hangs off it, and a safe pocket that falls on it moves to the room before. The room has exactly two doorways.

- **Layout** is `layOutStealthGateRoom` (`src/map/stealthGateLayout.ts`), returning `StealthGateRoomData` on `DungeonData` and `gameMap.stealthGateRoom`: `entranceDoorway`, `onwardDoorway` (the one the gate shuts, toward Big Rooster's gateway safe room), `gateTiles`, `guardTile`/`guardTalkTiles`, `prisonDoor` (a `PrisonDoorSpan`: two-tile double door painted on plain north wall face, entered from its `approachTiles` through the hand-made `BuildingEntry` from `prisonBuildingEntry`, type `'prison'`), the PRISON `signTile`/`signArrow`, the companions' `waitingTiles`, and `keepClearTiles` that no hallway spawn, sign or dressing may take.
- **Blocking** is a runtime flag: `BLOCK_STEALTH_GATE` (in `GameMap.ts`) on every gate tile (`stealthGateBlockedTiles`), honoured while `gameMap.stealthGateState()` is `'locked'`. `setStealthGate(state)` swaps the tile type between `STEALTH_GATE_CLOSED` and `STEALTH_GATE_OPEN` and marks the tiles dirty (base tiles are chunk-baked); `GameMapCheckpoint.stealthGate` carries it through a rewind. Unlike the bathroom queue, the gate also stops shots in flight: `isWalkableIgnoringBathroomQueue` still honours it.
- **`isWalkableIgnoringPuzzleChokes`** is `isWalkable` with the bathrooms fixed and the gate open. Use it for questions about the route as built (which rooms lie past a safe room, `pastSafeRoom.ts`), whose answer must not change when a puzzle is solved; never for where a crawler may step.
- **Progression:** S7 in `validateProgression` (expected through `hasStealthGate` in `progressionExpectations`): the gate room is the halfway chain room, has two doorways, generates shut, every route to the spine's gateway safe room passes through it, that gateway is unreachable while locked but the guard's talk tiles and the prison door are not, and the nursery, lanes, lab and pockets all stay off it.
- **The prison** is a `BuildingInteriorScene` built as the `goblin_prison` `InteriorVariant` (`plan: PrisonPlan`) by `GameMap.generateGoblinPrison`. Its floor plans are authored in `src/map/goblinPrisonLayouts.ts` (`PRISON_LAYOUTS`: `stockade`, `pit`, `keep`), each four sections walked in order (`intake`, `yard`, `cellBlock`, `vault`), with guards (`PrisonGuardDef.script`) and spotlights (`PrisonSpotlightTrack`, `path` or `pivot`) gated per difficulty by `PrisonTier`, every period dividing `cycleFrames`. Rows use the `PRISON_CHAR` glyphs: `#` wall, `.` floor, `%` tall cover (stops a flashlight, not a spotlight), `=` bars, `~` prisoner-cell floor, `,` holding-cell floor, `+` a holding cell's flimsy door, `$` key pedestal, `@` entry, `&` exit, `1`–`9` spotlight tower anchors in walls, and letters as named floor points the scripts use. A guard script is a loop starting on a named point: `>b` walk, `p40` pause, `s60/120` sweep, `l30` look back, `fN`/`fE`/`fS`/`fW` face. `src/map/goblinPrisonPlan.ts` parses one (`parsePrisonLayout`), picks the world's layout from the seed (`pickPrisonLayout`) and builds the `PrisonPlan` (`planGoblinPrison(worldSeed, difficulty, layout?)`) that the map, the stealth systems and the solver all read. The runtime half is in `game-architecture`.
- **Dev:** `npm run playtest -- prison` (spawn `stealthGateRoom`, in front of the guard), `?prison=<layout>&difficulty=<d>` (a 1-based index or layout id; boots straight into the prison).
- **Gates:** `npm run verify:stealth-gate` (S7 on generated floors plus the live map: shut while locked without shutting out the guard or door, open once unlocked with its art, checkpoint, door entry and sign, keep-clear), `verify:prison-layouts` (every authored layout parses, connects, keeps light out of the intake, and patrols and discs stay on floor), `verify:prison-stealth` (the solver in `scripts/prisonStealthSolver.ts`: every layout × difficulty has an unseen route to the key and back, a straight walk is caught, no route skips the yard or cell block, noise responses), `verify:progression`, `render:stealth-gate`, `render:goblin-prison`. Each `verify:` takes `--fault=` and must go red on every fault.

## Tiles

- Constants in `src/map/tileTypes.ts`: floor types via the `FLOOR_TYPES` array; everything else a numbered constant. A map cell is `TileContent { tileId, type, spriteKey?, decorationVariant? }`.
- Rendering: `TileRenderer.drawTile` tries category renderers in order — `terrainTiles` → `specialFloorTiles` → `buildingTiles` → `decorationTiles` → `interiorTiles` → `bossRoomTiles` (each returns `true` when handled; first match wins). `bossRoomTiles.ts` dispatches to one painter per room in `src/map/tiles/bossRooms/`.
- Ground: floor tiles delegate to `drawGroundTile` (`src/map/tiles/groundTiles.ts`), which resolves a `GroundMaterial` to a frame of the generated tileset and runs the fringe/tone/scatter/AO passes inside the chunk cache. Adding or tuning a material is the `add-ground-tile` skill, not a new `case`.
- Walkability: a **negative check** — a tile type is walkable unless listed in `NON_WALKABLE_TILE_TYPES` (`src/map/walkability.ts`), which `GameMap.isWalkable` reads via `isWalkableTileType` after its block-flag checks.
- Sight: a solid tile blocks sight unless it is in `SIGHT_TRANSPARENT_TILE_TYPES` (same file). `GameMap.hasLineOfSight` sees over those low props; code that steers a body straight at a point must ask `GameMap.hasWalkableLine`.
- Pace: ground that slows a crawler is listed in `src/map/tileSpeed.ts` and applied by `footingSpeedFactor` in `GameLoopPhases.applyMovement`.
- Boss-room tiles (`TRASH_*`, `GYM_*`, `POWER_LAB_*`, `LAB_*`) are grouped in `tileTypes.ts` as `BOSS_ROOM_PROP_TILE_TYPES` (solid, Y-sorted — spread into both decoration registries in `GameMap.ts` and `TileRenderer.ts`) and `BOSS_ROOM_FLAT_TILE_TYPES` (walkable decals baked into the chunk). The rooms that stamp them are described under "Boss-room dressing" in `game-architecture`; `npm run gates:boss-rooms` checks each type walks, blocks sight and sits in the registries as declared. `FLOODED_PUMP_ROOM_FLOOR` is the Kraken room's walkable floor tile (ordinary dungeon concrete under the standing water), and `GYM_KETTLEBELL_RACK` is a knee-high solid prop in the gym that does not block sight (it is in `SIGHT_TRANSPARENT_TILE_TYPES`).

### New tile type checklist

1. Add a numbered constant in `tileTypes.ts`; add it to `SHADOW_TYPES`/`NON_FLOOR_TYPES` in `src/map/tiles/helpers.ts` if opaque.
2. Add a `case` in the right `src/map/tiles/*` category renderer.
3. If it blocks movement, add it to `NON_WALKABLE_TILE_TYPES` in `src/map/walkability.ts`; walkable tiles need no change. If it is solid but low enough to see over, also add it to `SIGHT_TRANSPARENT_TILE_TYPES`.
4. If it is drawn in the Y-sorted pass (anything that stands up), it also needs the decoration registries in `TileRenderer.ts` and `GameMap.ts`, and the minimap colours. A missed entry renders bare floor and still typechecks. The full list is under "Tile types and registries" in the "Circus grounds" section of `docs/town.md`.

Finish with the `dev-workflow` gates (typecheck, lint, format).
