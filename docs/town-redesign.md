# Town Redesign — Making the Over City Read as a Lived-In Town

Plan for rebuilding the third-floor overworld town so it feels like a real, compact,
inhabited settlement instead of buildings scattered across a lawn.

> Progress tracker: [town-redesign-progress.md](town-redesign-progress.md)
> Source-material background: [over-city-reference.md](over-city-reference.md)

---

## 1. Why it doesn't read as a town today

Every number below was measured from `src/map/OverworldGenerator.ts` and
`src/images/environment/tilesets/overworld_tileset.png`, not eyeballed.

### 1.1 It is far too spread out

Building anchors span **74 × 73 tiles**. The 16 buildings occupy 890 of the 5402
tiles in that bounding box — **16.5% built density**. A real village is 35–50%.

The screen is 32 px/tile with no zoom, so a 1280 × 720 window shows ~40 × 22 tiles.
The town is roughly **four screens** of mostly grass. You are never looking at more
than three or four buildings at once, and there is nothing between them.

Current layout (each letter is one building, `.` is grass, blank is road):

### 1.2 The road network is a `+`, not a street plan

`generateOverworld` paints exactly two 5-tile roads through the map centre, a 22 × 22
road slab for the square, and a per-door stub for each building. There are no blocks,
no frontage, no junctions, no alleys, no hierarchy. Everything that isn't a road or a
building is undifferentiated grass, including the interior of the town.

### 1.3 The ground tileset cannot tile, and no amount of code will make it

`overworld_tileset.png` was generated by ChatGPT. Diffusion models have no mechanism
for edge continuity, so the sheet is a **grid of individual illustrations**, not
tiles. Ryan hand-repaired the handful the game actually uses; the rest were never
usable. Three independent measurements confirm this.

**(a) Most tiles carry a baked-in dark frame.** Border luminance minus interior
luminance — a tileable texture scores ~0, a framed illustration scores strongly
negative:

| row               | c0        | c1        | c2        | c3    | c4    | c6    | c11   |
| ----------------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| r0 `grass`        | **+2.7**  | **+1.4**  | **+0.4**  | −21.6 | −33.7 | −24.4 | −15.3 |
| r3 `village_streets` | **+1.8** | **+0.1** | **+2.7**  | −35.0 | −49.5 | −39.2 | −26.5 |
| r4 `sand`         | −26.8     | −34.7     | −40.1     | −58.2 | −61.0 | −60.4 | −49.8 |
| r6 `cobblestone`  | −15.8     | −24.9     | −26.6     | −40.8 | −41.5 | −25.8 | −17.5 |
| r7 `tile`         | −26.8     | −40.0     | −39.5     | −51.0 | −49.4 | −42.8 | −40.7 |

Only **row 0 cols 0–2** and **row 3 cols 0–2** are frame-free — exactly the tiles that
were hand-repaired, and exactly the ones the game draws. Every other tile would paint
a dark grid onto the ground. **Rows 4–10 (sand, gravel, cobblestone, tile, overgrown,
stone, concrete) are unusable in their entirety**, column 0 included.

**(b) Even the repaired tiles don't truly tile.** Wrap error at the joint (a genuinely
seamless tile scores 0–3):

| Tile                       | left↔right | top↔bottom |
| -------------------------- | ---------- | ---------- |
| grass col0                 | 6.3        | 5.8        |
| grass col1                 | 3.3        | **9.8**    |
| grass col2                 | 4.3        | **9.2**    |
| grass 3-wide strip as a whole | 7.1     | 8.3        |
| streets col0               | **10.2**   | 0.2        |
| streets col1               | **10.8**   | 0.2        |

The repair work made the strip blend *left-to-right within itself*, but nothing
wraps. The street tiles happen to be vertically seamless (0.2) and horizontally
broken (~10) — which is why the ground reads as **squares if you don't rotate, and
crosses if you do**. Rotation doesn't fix a discontinuity, it just changes which axis
it lands on.

**(c) The sheet isn't even on an integer grid.** The image is 768 × **703** px, so the
true row pitch is 63.909 while `manifest.json` declares `frameHeight: 64`. Every row
below the first drifts, accumulating ~0.9 px by row 10 — a tell that the sheet was
sliced out of a generated image on a non-integer grid.

**Consequence:** the ground art has to be replaced, not re-used harder. See §7 — this
is the foundation the rest of the visual work sits on.

The `overworldRotation` call in `src/map/tiles/terrainTiles.ts` is still worth
deleting (it inflates seam error from ~6 to ~19–31 on tiles that were repaired to butt
together horizontally), but on its own it only trades one artifact for another.

### 1.4 Buildings are stickers on a lawn

The building PNGs are transparent front elevations with no ground attached. They are
placed straight onto grass with no plot, no dirt apron, no doorstep, no contact
shadow, no fence, no yard. Nothing visually connects a building to the ground it
stands on, and no two buildings share a frontage line.

### 1.5 The tower forces the sprawl

`overworld_main_tower` is 6 × 23 tiles of art but only blocks its bottom two rows. Its
spire overhangs 22 tiles of ground to the north, so anything placed there is hidden
behind it. Placing the tower in the *middle* of town (current anchor `cy − 15`) sterilises
a 6 × 22 corridor through the town centre and pushes the north buildings outward. This
is a large, non-obvious driver of the current spread.

### 1.6 Nothing occupies the negative space

No fences, hedges, gardens, carts, crates, laundry lines, lampposts, hanging signs,
planters, troughs, hay, signposts, or kerbs. The prop library is `torch`, `well`,
`fountain`, `barrel`, `barrel_side`, `crate`, `brazier` — and only the first three are
used outdoors.

## 2. Design principles

1. **Compact beats large.** Target the whole town inside one-and-a-half screens.
   Nothing should be more than ~28 tiles from the plaza.
2. **Streets define blocks; blocks define buildings.** Place streets first, then hang
   buildings off street frontages, shoulder to shoulder. Never place a building in
   open space.
3. **Every surface is a decision.** Plaza flagstone, main-street cobble, side-lane
   packed street, alley dirt, yard gravel, field grass. Bare "default grass" only
   exists outside the walls.
4. **Buildings own ground.** Every building gets a plot: apron, doorstep, contact
   shadow, and either a yard or a shared party line with its neighbour.
5. **Negative space is designed too.** The gaps between buildings are gardens, alleys,
   stableyards and drying greens — not leftover lawn.
6. **Give every place a reason to exist.** A landmark you navigate by, a thing to do,
   or an NPC who belongs there.
7. **Preserve the story.** Every existing building, name, quest hook and system anchor
   survives the move. This is a re-composition, not a re-scoping.

## 3. The redesign

### 3.1 Ground rendering — the biggest single visual win

This rests on a **new, generated ground tileset** (§7). The current sheet cannot
deliver it (§1.3). Changes land in `src/map/tiles/terrainTiles.ts` plus a new material
module.

**A. Replace the ground tileset.** Generated offline, torus-sampled so every tile
wraps against itself and against every other variant of its material. See §7 for the
method and the proof. `overworldRotation` is deleted outright — with tiles that
genuinely wrap, rotation buys nothing and can only reintroduce discontinuity.

**B. Many variants per material, no repeat pattern.** Generation is cheap, so ship
8–12 variants per material and pick per tile by hash. Because they all wrap against
each other, any shuffle is valid — no strip indexing, no adjacency rules, no
hand-repair.

**C. Expand from 2 materials to 7.** Introduce a `GroundMaterial` enum. Each material
becomes a row of the new sheet:

| Material      | Used for                                | Notes                                    |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| `PLAZA_FLAG`  | market plaza, temple terrace            | large cut slabs, worn joints             |
| `MAIN_COBBLE` | Market Street, King's Road              | rounded setts, cart ruts, wheel polish   |
| `LANE_STREET` | side lanes, building frontages          | smaller mixed setts, mud between stones  |
| `YARD_GRAVEL` | workyards, stableyard, gate aprons      | loose chip, scattered larger stones      |
| `ALLEY_DIRT`  | alleys, back paths, farm tracks         | packed earth, puddles, footprints        |
| `VERGE`       | street verges, wall bases, garden edges | grass invaded by stone and weeds         |
| `FIELD_GRASS` | outside the walls, gardens, greens      | the PoC in §7                            |

**D. Soften every material boundary.** Two cheap techniques, both computed inside the
existing 16-tile chunk cache (`TileRenderer`) so there is no per-frame cost:

- _Fringe pass_ — where a tile's 4-neighbour is a different material, draw a
  noise-masked sliver of the neighbouring material over the shared edge, so the border
  wanders instead of running dead straight.
- _Scatter pass_ — grass tufts spill a few pixels onto street tiles adjacent to grass;
  loose cobbles and mud scatter onto dirt adjacent to cobble.

**E. Low-frequency ground variation — required, not optional.** One octave of value
noise (wavelength ~24 tiles, ±6% brightness) multiplied over ground tiles, sampled in
**world space** so it does not repeat per tile.

This is the one artifact per-tile seamlessness does *not* solve: if each tile carries
its own large-scale tonal pattern, the variants themselves read as blocks even though
the edges are perfect. The §7 proof of concept shows exactly this — the grass is
flawless, the dirt shows faint tonal blocking. A world-space layer on top is the fix,
and it must ship together with the new tileset, not after it.

**F. Ambient occlusion.** A soft dark gradient on ground tiles adjacent to walls,
buildings, and the wall ring. Ground currently meets vertical surfaces with zero
shading, which is a large part of the "pasted on" look.

### 3.2 Town shape — a walled market village

Replace the `+` crossroads with a hierarchy:

- **Wall ring** (~55 × 40 inside) with **three gates**: south (King's Road, the main
  arrival), west and east on Market Street. The wall gives the town an edge, makes
  "inside" mean something, and gives the ruins outside a purpose.
- **King's Road** — 4 tiles wide, enters the south gate and runs north to Market
  Street. The arrival sightline: you come through the gate and see the tower rising
  over the plaza at the far end.
- **Market Street** — 4 tiles wide, west gate to east gate, along the plaza's south
  edge. The town's commercial spine.
- **Upper Lane / Cross Lane / Low Street** — 3 tiles wide, east–west, defining four
  building bands.
- **West Lane / East Lane** — 3 tiles wide, north–south connectors.
- **Alleys** — 2 tiles wide, cutting through blocks. Shortcuts that reward
  exploration, and a proper home for the murder-mystery body behind the club.
- **Market Plaza** — flagstone, at the town's heart, with the tower's civic terrace on
  its north side.

**Move the tower to the town's north edge.** Its 22-tile spire then rises over the
*wall and the fields beyond*, hiding nothing, and becomes a skyline landmark visible
from the south gate. This alone recovers a 6 × 22 dead corridor from the town centre.

### 3.4 Plots and frontage

Every sprite building gains a **plot** painted before the building:

1. **Apron** — 1–2 rows of `LANE_STREET` in front of the facade, wider than the door.
2. **Doorstep** — the existing threshold slab, kept.
3. **Contact shadow** — a soft ellipse under the facade's base row.
4. **Party line or yard** — buildings 1 tile apart share a wall line; buildings 3+
   tiles apart get a fenced yard with material `YARD_GRAVEL` and props.
5. **Back garden** — the interior of each block becomes gardens and drying greens
   reached from the alleys, not dead grass.

Plots also fix a downstream nuisance: ground scatter currently paints weeds under
building art (`isUnderSpriteBuilding` suppresses it only per-sprite).

### 3.5 Props and signage

The codebase already draws props procedurally and well (`TownPropSystem`'s notice
board, benches and fortune teller; the market's stalls). Extend that pattern rather
than commissioning art. New procedural props, roughly in value order:

**Tier 1 — sells "town" immediately**

- **Hanging shop signs** on wrought brackets over every named door (mug, anvil, mortar
  & pestle, cart wheel, moon, needle). Identify any building at a glance.
- **Fences and gates** — picket, post-and-rail, wattle. Enclose yards and gardens.
- **Lampposts / hanging lanterns** along Market Street and the Low Quarter, lit.
- **Kerbs and gutters** along street edges — a 4 px stone lip that makes a street a
  street rather than a strip of different tiles.

**Tier 2 — clutter and life**

- Handcarts, wagons, wagon wheels leaning on walls
- Crate and barrel stacks, sacks, hay bales
- Laundry lines strung across alleys
- Planters, window boxes, herb beds, vegetable rows
- Water trough, hitching post, chicken coop
- Smithy: anvil, coal pile, quench barrel, tool rack
- Wells and pumps in the block gardens

**Tier 3 — wayfinding**

- **Signposts** at every gate and street junction with district names
- **Gate arches** framing the three gates
- **Banners and bunting** on the civic terrace and across Market Street

### 3.6 Life and navigation

- **Retune `TownLifeSystem`** — `PLAZA_RADIUS_TILES` 20 → ~11, `DISTRICT_RADIUS_TILES`
  48 → ~26, matching the compacted town. Today these radii mean citizens wander into
  empty fields.
- **Bias wander targets to street tiles** so townsfolk walk lanes and cross the plaza
  instead of drifting over grass and through gardens.
- **Activity anchors** — stationary citizens tied to props: someone drawing water at a
  well, a smith at the anvil, a hawker at a stall, kids on the fountain steps, a
  bouncer at the club door.
- **Shrink `TOWN_SAFE_RADIUS_TILES`** 55 → ~40 (the farthest wall corner is ~37 tiles
  out). That widens the ruins band between the wall and the circus, so leaving town
  actually feels like leaving town.
- **Retune ambient audio radii** in `DungeonScene.buildTownAmbientEmitters` to the new
  plaza and town extents.
- **District labels on the minimap** (`MiniMapSystem`).

## 5. Code architecture

`generateOverworld` is a single 709-line function holding street geometry, building
anchors, decoration, spawn scatter and metadata. Adding a real street plan to it in
place will not end well. Extract a declarative layer:

```
src/map/town/
  townPlan.ts         TownPlan data: districts, street polylines, plots, prop slots
  paintGround.ts      material regions → tile types; verges; gate aprons
  paintStreets.ts     rasterise street polylines with width, kerbs, junction fillets
  paintPlots.ts       per-building apron, doorstep, contact shadow, yard, fence
  groundMaterials.ts  GroundMaterial → tileset row/frame; transition + scatter rules
  townProps.ts        procedural prop registry (signs, fences, lamps, carts, …)
```

`OverworldGenerator.generateOverworld(size)` keeps its signature and its
`OverworldData` return contract; it consumes a `TownPlan` and keeps ownership of the
circus, forests, ruins and spawn scatter (all unchanged).

Rendering changes stay in `src/map/tiles/terrainTiles.ts` and `TileRenderer`'s cached
ground pass. Nothing new runs per frame.

**Type-safety note:** street polylines, materials and prop kinds should all be
discriminated unions with `as const` tables — no casts, no non-null assertions, and
numeric literals extracted into named constants per `CLAUDE.md`.

## 6. Invariants that must not break

These are load-bearing for quests, systems and save state. Anything that moves must be
re-derived, not hard-coded twice.

**Names.** All 16 `buildingEntries` names and `type` values, verbatim. Quests, dialog,
interiors, mercenaries, the club, the murder mystery and the cult hideout all key off
these strings across ~20 files.

**`OverworldData` fields.** `startTile`, `buildingEntries`, `mainTowerAnchor`,
`doomsdayEscapeTile`, `townSquareCentre`, `fountainCentre`, `circusCentre`,
`circusRadiusTiles`, `townSafeRadiusTiles`, `hallwaySpawnPoints`.

**Story geometry.**

- Tower base must stay adjacent to the plaza (magistrate's office, tower stairs).
- `doomsdayEscapeTile` stays just south of the tower door and out of `stairwellTiles`.
- The circus stays 70–90 tiles from centre, outside the safe radius, with a ruins
  buffer.
- Miller's Farm, Blackwood Lodge (cult hideout) and the club alley (GumGum's body) must
  each remain reachable and thematically placed.

**Centre-relative offsets that must be re-tuned, not left stale.**

| Consumer                    | Constants                                                            |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `market/vendorDefs.ts`      | `WEST_STALL_DX`, `EAST_STALL_DX`, `STALL_ROW_DY`                      |

caching rules stay as they are.

## 7. The new ground tileset

**Yes, the ground art must be replaced**, and it should be **generated by a script in
this repo**, not prompted from an image model.

### Why generation wins here

Tileability is not an aesthetic property, it is a **mathematical** one: the pixels at
x = 63 must be the neighbours of the pixels at x = 0. An image model has no mechanism
to satisfy that constraint, which is why §1.3 measures what it measures, and why
hand-repairing each tile is expensive and only ever gets you *close*.

Sampling noise on a **torus** — a lattice whose coordinates wrap modulo the tile size —
makes seamlessness true by construction. There is nothing to repair, nothing to check,
and it holds for every variant against every other variant of the same material, so
variants can be shuffled freely.

### Status: built

The generator is **landed**, not proposed. `npx tsx scripts/generate-ground-tileset.ts`
produces `ground_overworld.png`, `ground_dungeon.png` and `ground_masks.png` plus
their manifest entries. Review in-game at `localhost:8080/?tiles`. The
`add-ground-tile` skill is the working reference.

Fourteen materials ship today:

| Sheet      | Materials                                                                                     |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| overworld  | grass, verge, dirt, gravel, lane, cobble, plaza                                                 |
| dungeon    | plain (calm, jointless), flagstone, worn, mossy, wet, rubble, wall                               |

### Method

```
scripts/tilegen/noise.ts      NoiseField — value/fbm/worley/warp, all torus-wrapped
scripts/tilegen/raster.ts     Surface + wrapping primitives (disc, stroke, chunk)
scripts/tilegen/palette.ts    ramps anchored to the art that already works
scripts/tilegen/materials.ts  the painters
scripts/tilegen/masks.ts      16 corner transition masks
scripts/tilegen/sheet.ts      patch slicing, sheet packing, manifest, seam audit
```

Four things turned out to matter more than the noise itself:

**1. Generate multi-tile patches, not tiles.** A material is painted as one
wrapped field covering 2x2 or 4x4 tiles and sliced afterwards, so the pattern's
repeat period is the patch. Wrapping at a single tile forces every feature to be
smaller than a tile, which is how you end up with a hard joint every 16 screen
pixels and a floor that reads as a grid.

**2. Wrapping makes a patch seamless against itself, not against a sibling.**
Two variants of a paved material seeded differently will not line up where they
meet. Geometry therefore comes from a `structure` seed shared across a material's
variants; only tints, wear and scatter use the per-variant `detail` seed.

**3. Joint contrast is a budget.** Every material in the first draft had hard
paving joints, so nothing could hold a stretch of floor without becoming a grid.
`dungeon_plain` is the answer — calm, jointless, mineral-grained stone for the
bulk of a room, with the jointed variants used for edges and accents.

**4. Keep large-scale tone out of the tile.** A low broad-noise period puts one
tonal sweep inside each patch, so patches read as blocks even when their pixels
match perfectly. That belongs in the renderer's world-space layer (§3.1E).

### Seam audit, built into the build

The generator measures each patch's wrap joints against **its own strongest
internal edges** and exits non-zero above a ratio of 1.15. Absolute edge
difference is the wrong yardstick — a slab material legitimately contains hard
lines, and its patch edge may land on one. Current worst across all 14 materials:
**1.11**.

### Transitions

Sixteen corner masks, indexed by which of a tile's four corners belong to the
upper material. Corners rather than edges is what buys diagonals: a tile with only
its NW corner set draws a curved wedge, not an axis-aligned half.

The masks ship as one shared sheet and are composited at load, not baked per
material pair — baking would need a row per pair *per patch phase*, and would fix
at build time which pairs a level may blend. Compositing instead means any
material can meet any other, on any floor, at any patch alignment, from sixteen
64x64 frames.

The mask seed depends only on the corner bits, never on tile position: two
neighbouring tiles must perturb their shared boundary identically or the edge
tears.

### Risk, stated honestly

Procedural ground can look sterile if the palettes and feature painters are bad.
Seamlessness is guaranteed; attractiveness is iteration. Two materials needed a
second pass already — dirt showed per-tile tonal blocking, and rubble read as
polka dots until chunks got overlapping lobes and contact shadows. Expect more of
that, and use `?tiles` to catch it.

### Art still worth commissioning

Generation is right for **ground**. It is the wrong tool for **objects**. Where new
drawn art would pay off, in order: a **town wall + gate arch** sheet, a **market
stall/awning** sheet, and **fence/hedge** tiles — all absent today and all hard to fake
at 32 px. Everything else in §3.5 suits the codebase's existing procedural-prop style.
The building PNGs are good and are not being touched.

## 9. Acceptance criteria

The redesign is done when all of these hold:

- [ ] Standing at the south gate, the tower, Market Street and the plaza are all
      visible in one frame.
- [ ] Any two adjacent buildings are either sharing a party line or separated by a
      *purposeful* space (yard, alley, garden) — never bare grass.
- [ ] No visible tile grid on any large ground area at 1× zoom.
- [ ] Every named building is identifiable without walking to its door.
- [ ] Walking plaza → any building door takes under 12 seconds at `PLAYER_SPEED`.
- [ ] All 16 buildings enterable; every quest that references a building still starts,
      progresses and completes.
- [ ] Circus quest, murder mystery, cult hideout, club, market, mercenary guild and the
      Doomsday escape all verified end to end.
- [ ] `npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint` exit 0; `npm run format` clean.
- [ ] No frame-time regression on the overworld versus the current build.

| --------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A stale centre-relative offset drops a stall/bench/board into a wall | Phase 1 refactor first; §6 table is the checklist; `?townmap` shows it instantly |
